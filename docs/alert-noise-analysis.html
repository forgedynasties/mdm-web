Why the top four are noise

Each of them fires on a normal operating condition, at a line healthy devices sit on, with no minimum duration and no gap between resolving and firing again.

What the engine can and cannot do today

The plan in the last review assumed the engine would grow hysteresis, cooldown and muting. That work has not happened, so it is worth stating exactly where the code stands before planning on top of it again.

CapabilityStateDetail
Minimum durationpartialHand-written per rule for five of nineteen (temp_elevated, memory_low, wifi_weak, wlc_continuous, slow_charge_night). The rest fire on a single reading.
Resolve hysteresisnoneResolve uses the same threshold as fire, so a device on the line oscillates. No "clear for N minutes" anywhere.
Cooldown / occurrence countnoneThe occurrences column exists but is deliberately never incremented and never rendered. Once a row resolves, the next episode is a brand-new row — that is where 51 rows per device for one rule come from.
Flapper detection, snooze, mutenoneThe only way to silence a rule is for an admin to disable it fleet-wide in Settings.
Offline suppressionpartialMost point-in-time rules require a check-in in the last 15 min, which suppresses them by accident. device_crash, charger_flapping and the daily-tier rules (memory_pressure, storage_filling, wlc_dead) have no such guard — which is why wlc_dead reads "pad never engaged" for devices that were simply off.
offline vs offline_peaktwo rowsSeparate rules on a (type, device) unique index, so a placed device offline at peak gets a warning row and a critical row. No escalation in place.
Crash loopnoneAny crash in 15 minutes is a critical. One device crashed 32 times this week and never produced anything stronger than 32 separate criticals.
Severity routingpartialPer-channel min_severity works, but the Teams channel is set to warning, so 9,062 of 9,074 alerts were posted. A channel set to digest mode is never delivered at all: the only digest job is the daily AI fleet summary on the legacy webhook.
Per-site rulesnonescope_type / scope_id exist on the table, but both evaluators skip any rule that is not fleet-wide.
Alert quality metricsnoneThe page shows open counts only. Every number in this document came from SQL.

"Clear all" still hard-deletes. It is DELETE FROM alerts, taking resolved history with it, and anything still true re-fires within the minute. "Resolve all" and "Ack all" do the right thing — they are status updates that keep history — but no button in the UI points at them.

What an alert should be

Unchanged from the last review, and worth repeating because the current set fails it: an alert is a condition a named person can do something about, that lasts long enough to be looked at, and that is stated once.

SeverityQualifiesGoes to
criticalA placed device offline during service. Crash loop: 3 in an hour. Storage under 1 GB. Battery under 10% and not charging at peak.Teams, realtime, and the page
warningSustained real heat, low storage, low memory, misplaced pad, slow overnight charge.The page, and one 08:00 digest
infoNew device onboarded, update landed, kiosk mode changed.Activity log only

Next steps in order

  1. Settings only, today applied 16 Sep 09:19 UTCTeams channel to critical — this alone stops 3,878 warning posts a week. Overheating off-pad to 48°C (698 of 4,033 episodes survive). Temp elevated band to 42–48°C. Wi-Fi weak to −80 dBm sustained 20 min. Wireless-charging continuous to 4 hours, or off until it has a purpose. Nothing to build; all of it is Settings › Alerts.
    settings
  2. Fold repeats into one rowBump occurrences and reopen the existing row when the same alert returns within 30 minutes of resolving, instead of inserting a new one. Render the count. This is the single highest-leverage change: it turns 9,074 rows into about 1,088 without changing a single threshold.
    build
  3. Hysteresis and duration as engine featuresFire only after the condition holds N minutes; resolve only after it has been clear M minutes and past a lower bound. Today five rules hand-roll half of this and fourteen have none. Same two params for every threshold rule.
    build
  4. Fix the duplicates and the dependentsFold offline_peak into offline as an escalation of one row. Suppress dependent alerts while a device is not reporting, explicitly rather than by accident — that removes the false wlc_dead. Group the overnight trio behind one "charging overnight" condition.
    build
  5. Flapping and snoozeMore than five episodes a day becomes one flapping alert, muted for that type until an operator resolves it. Snooze per alert, device or type. Make "Clear all" resolve-and-snooze for 24 h instead of deleting history.
    build
  6. Crash loopThree crashes in an hour is a critical, once, with the count on the row. Anything less stays a warning on the device page. The device that crashed 32 times should have produced one alert, not 32.
    build
  7. Routing that means somethingCritical to Teams in realtime, warnings in one 08:00 digest — and make digest-mode channels actually deliver, since today they silently drop. Info to the activity log. Per-site recipients and quiet hours after that.
    build
  8. Measure it on the pageAn alert-quality panel: alerts per day, share resolved under 10 minutes, top flappers, acknowledged versus ignored. Every table in this document, on screen, so the next review does not need a database.
    build
