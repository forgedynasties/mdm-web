Why overheating is noise

The rule fires the moment battery temperature reads at or above 45°C and auto-resolves the minute it reads below. The readings that fired were 45.0, 45.5 and 45.7 against a limit of 45. A device hovering at the line fires, resolves, fires again, every few minutes for the whole service.

The rule today

  • Fire when the latest reading is ≥ 45°C off the pad, ≥ 65°C on the pad
  • Resolve the next minute the reading is below
  • No minimum duration, no hysteresis, no cooldown
  • Each episode is a new row, so one device made 95 rows

Evidence

  • Median time open: 1 minute
  • 99% resolved before anyone could open them
  • Summaries read "46°C off pad (limit 45°C)"
  • Healthy T7s sit at 44 to 46°C during service

The crash rule is better built: a 15 minute window and one row per episode. But a device that crashes every window still produces a fresh alert each time instead of "this device has crashed 14 times today".

What is on and what is off

On

  • overheating 45 / 65°C the noisy one
  • storage_low 5 GB
  • storage_filling 2 GB drop
  • storage_warning 14 GB
  • memory_low 400 MB
  • new_device
  • battery_low 20% at peak
  • device_crash 15 min window

Off

  • offline 30 min should be on
  • offline_peak 5 min should be on
  • wifi_weak, wifi_unstable
  • charger_flapping, wlc_dead, wlc_continuous
  • slow_charge_night, battery_high_night
  • temp_elevated, memory_pressure

Turning off "offline" removed the one alert an operator can always act on, while the rule producing 86% of the volume stayed on. The Teams channel receives warning and above in realtime, so every overheating blip reached the channel too.

What an alert should be

An alert is a condition that a named person can do something about, that lasts long enough to be looked at, and that is stated once. Everything else is telemetry and belongs on the device page, not in the inbox.

The plan

  1. Hysteresis and duration on every threshold ruleFire when the condition has held for N minutes (overheating: 5). Resolve only after it has been clear for M minutes (10) and below a lower bound (42°C). One episode, one alert.
    build
  2. Cooldown per device per typeThe same alert on the same device within 30 minutes of resolving reopens the old row and bumps its count. The list reads "746 overheated 6 times today", one line.
    build
  3. Overheating limit 48°C off pad45°C is where healthy T7s sit during service. Keep 65°C on the wireless pad.
    setting
  4. Offline back on, with a real threshold15 minutes during service hours, 60 minutes outside. Offline is the alert restaurants feel.
    setting
  5. Severity that routesOnly critical goes to Teams in realtime. Warnings stay on the page. Info goes to the activity log.
    build
  6. Flapper detectionA device that fires the same alert more than 5 times in a day gets one "flapping" alert and is muted for that type until an operator resolves it. Would have turned 95 alerts into 1.
    build
  7. Daily digest for warningsOne Teams post at 8 am: "3 devices worth a look", linking to the alerts page.
    build
  8. Stop clearing the inboxWith 1 to 6 in place the list stays short by itself, and history survives for trend analysis.
    habit
