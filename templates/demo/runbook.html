Demo run-of-show

~25 min total Audience: mixed / external Live on the real fleet 9 segments + Q&A
Say this first — the throughline

“It already runs our 900-device fleet, and it does the hard things well.”

Everything you show maps back to this one sentence: real fleet, real control, and a clear plan. Lead with the product moving on screen — not slides.

DO NOT RESTART
Freeze the server for the whole window

The platform is single-instance — a restart or redeploy drops every live device connection and your “live” demo goes cold. No docker compose, no deploys, from T-60 until you're done.

EXTERNAL IN ROOM
Keep the candor internal

With outside people present, do not open the audit page or walk the readiness gaps. Security = the positive pen-test story only. Frame the roadmap as ambition, not as a list of what's missing.

T-60 minutes

Pre-flight checklist

Walk this an hour before. A cold device or a stray notification is what actually kills a live demo.

  • Confirm 2–3 demo devices are online — open /devices, verify green / WS-connected. Pick your “hero” device for remote control.
  • Wake the hero device and keep it on power so it doesn't sleep or throttle its check-in mid-demo.
  • Server freeze confirmed — tell anyone with deploy access: no restarts during your slot.
  • Backup recordings ready — a 15-sec clip each of remote control and an OTA rollout, in case live gets flaky.
  • Tabs pre-opened & logged in, in order: Overview → Fleet Health → a device → Releases → Wrapped. Full-screen the browser.
  • Silence notifications, hide bookmarks bar, zoom to a readable size for the room's screen.
  • Have a recent release/deployment ready to show — either start a small staged rollout live, or open a completed one.
  • Audit page is admin-gated — confirmed. Don't navigate to it while sharing your screen.

If flaky Give it 5 seconds; if the mirror doesn't paint, say “let me show you the recording” and play the clip. Don't fight it live.

Pre-loaded answers

The hard questions — answer them crisply

Confident, short, and always end on the plan. Never apologize your way through one.

Q“Is it secure?”
“We had an independent firm attack it. They found real issues; we fixed the serious ones within a month. The foundations — encrypted traffic, no injection, separated access — they specifically credited.”
Q“What happens if the server goes down?”
“Today it's a single instance — solid at our current scale. Making it fault-tolerant with zero-downtime deploys is an explicit item on the plan, and we know exactly how to do it.”
Q“What if we lose the data?”
“Automated backups with point-in-time recovery are the first thing in the plan — I'm naming it before you have to. It's a known, funded line item, not a surprise.”
Q“How do you know it works — is it tested?”
“It's proven in the field on 900 devices daily. Formal automated testing is the top engineering investment I'm asking for, precisely so we can move faster with confidence.”
Q“Why build it instead of buying an MDM?”
“Off-the-shelf MDM can't do our hardware-specific pieces — the boot splash, the wireless-charging pad telemetry, our OTA and kiosk flow. This is built exactly around our devices and our operation.”
Q“Can it scale past 900?”
“The device agent and data model are built for it. The one ceiling is the single-instance server, and Phase 3 lifts it with a standard message-bus pattern. It's an upgrade, not a rebuild.”
The plan you're presenting

Phased path — and the concrete ask

Keep it to ambition and sequencing in front of external folks. The specifics of the gaps stay internal.

Phase 1 · Foundation
Rock-solid basics
  • Automated testing + continuous integration
  • Backups with point-in-time recovery
  • Graceful restarts & hardening
Phase 2 · Trust
Enterprise-grade
  • Per-device credentials & rotation
  • Extra approval on high-impact actions
  • Monitoring, metrics & runbooks
Phase 3 · Scale
Grow with confidence
  • Fault-tolerant, multi-node server
  • Handle far more than 900 devices
  • Round out alerting & automation
Your ask — fill this in before the meeting

e.g. “[N] engineers for [X] weeks to complete Phase 1, and a go / no-go decision on moving to full production rollout by [date].” End the meeting with a decision, not a recap.
