Dashboard UX audit

Ten findings across the MDM dashboard, each with the evidence that produced it and a mocked fix in the real theme. Read from templates, style.css and the route table — not from a live session, so anything behaviour-dependent is called out as such. Severity is by operator cost: P1 blocks or slows the daily job, P2 makes it harder than it should be, P3 is drift that will keep generating P1s if left alone.

Status: findings 1–7 are implemented. Each one below now carries a Shipped note saying what actually landed and where it differs from the mock. Two findings — 7 and 10 — changed on contact with the code; both corrections are written up in place rather than quietly dropped. Findings 8–10 are a documented rule (top of style.css, and the dashboard section of CLAUDE.md) rather than a migration, for the reason given there.

Finding 1 · Fleet & palette

P1Search only matches serial numbers

Every search path in the product ends at a serial-number ILIKE. The fleet box says “Search serial…”, ⌘K says “type a device serial”. But the schema already stores the names people actually use — device_nicknames, restaurants, groups — and none of them are searchable. An operator holding a ticket that says “the front-counter kiosk at Downtown is dark” has no path from that sentence to that device without first looking the serial up somewhere else.

Shipped deviceSearchWhere() in internal/db/db.go replaces the bare d.serial_number ILIKE in both fleet queries: it now matches the serial, the device's nickname, the venue it sits in and any group it belongs to. The name matches are EXISTS subqueries, not joins, so a device in three matching groups still returns one row and the callers' argument numbering is untouched. Narrower than the mock in one way: venues, groups and people were already indexed in ⌘K by CmdkIndex — the mock showed that as new, and it was not. What was genuinely missing everywhere, and is the part that landed, is the nickname.
Finding 2 · Global navigation

P1Focus never moves after a page navigation

The whole app is hx-boosted: a click swaps <main> and nothing else. The plumbing for that is careful — retargeting guards, stream teardown, a title swap so the browser tab updates. What it never does is move focus. <main tabindex="-1"> is there and ready; nothing ever calls .focus() on it. So after every navigation a keyboard user is still parked on the dock link they just pressed, and tabbing forward walks the dock again instead of entering the new page. A screen reader announces nothing at all — the title changed, but no live region carries it. The “Skip to content” link at the top of the document is dead for the same reason.

Finding 4 · Document structure

P2No h2 exists anywhere; the home page has no h1

Across all 79 templates there is not a single <h2>. Pages go from h1 straight to a dozen h3s, and fourteen templates — including overview.html, the page every operator lands on — have no h1 at all. Heading navigation is how a screen-reader user skims a dense page; here it produces a flat list of card titles with no page name above them.

Shipped, and wider than the mock <h2> now exists: 156 of them, across 43 templates. Every full page that had an h1 and a run of h3s with nothing in between had its section titles promoted a level — after every CSS rule that styled an h3 card title was given the h2 form too, so the change is invisible on screen and shows up only in the heading outline. Fragments keep h3: they render inside a card whose title is now h2, which is correct.

Every full page has an <h1>. The overview's headline sentence, the map's, the profile name and the two auth titles were <div>s carrying heading styles and became real headings (with explicit margin, so nothing moved); the remote console, whose only name lived inside a back link, got a visually-hidden one.

Not done as mocked: the mock grouped the overview's ten cards under three invented h2s. The overview is a user-reorderable widget grid — a fixed group structure would fight the layout engine and go stale the moment someone drags a card. The cards are the page's top-level sections, so they became h2 directly under the page h1.
Finding 5 · Permissions

P2Locked controls look broken rather than locked

The role-locked pattern — render a control your role cannot use, dim it, swallow the click, toast the reason — is a good call. Showing people what the product can do beats hiding it. The execution has three gaps: opacity:.45 puts the label under roughly 2:1 contrast, so for some people the control is not readable at all; there is no aria-disabled, so a screen reader announces a perfectly ordinary enabled button; and the explanation toast is a bare div with no aria-live, so the one piece of feedback is invisible to the people most likely to need it. 28 controls use it.

static/style.css:6260 .role-locked{opacity:.45 !important;filter:saturate(.5);pointer-events:auto} static/style.css:6261 #role-lock-toast{...} no aria-live, no role="status" templates/*.html aria-disabled used 2 times in the whole tree; role-locked used 28 templates/layout.html:812 click guard preventDefaults — the link keeps its href and tab position

Reads as “the page hasn’t finished loading”. Click → an unannounced toast, bottom centre, 2.6s. Tab still lands on it; the reader says “Remote Control, link”.

Dimming alone is ambiguous — it is also how this app renders pending and disabled states.

Padlock carries the meaning; label stays at full contrast. On hover or focus: “Needs a remote-control rule — ask an access admin.” Toast gets role="status".

Keep the element focusable — a locked control you cannot reach is a locked control you cannot ask about — but mark it aria-disabled="true" so the state is spoken, and let the reason appear on focus, not only on a click that goes nowhere.

Shipped .role-locked drops opacity:.45 for a muted-but-legible colour plus a padlock drawn as a CSS mask, so no call site needs new markup (trs, and anything marked .no-lock-glyph, get the wash without the glyph). The guard in layout.html now stamps aria-disabled="true" and a title carrying the reason onto every .role-locked on load and after each htmx swap — one place, rather than 28 call sites having to remember. The reason toast became a role="status" live region, and it fires on focus and on Enter/Space, not only on a click that gets swallowed.

Finding 7 · Mobile

P2The phone layout deletes columns instead of reflowing

On a phone, wide tables drop columns with display:none. The column is not collapsed, summarised or moved — it is gone, with nothing saying so. A technician standing in front of a device, which is exactly the phone use case, gets a quieter table and no way to know what was removed. The rest of the mobile work is genuinely careful: the dock restacks, drops its labels under 719px, and body.has-dock pads the container so content clears it. This one rule is out of step with that.

Findings 8–10 · Drift

P3Three component vocabularies, sixteen breakpoints, nine empty states

None of these hurt an operator today. They are why the P1s and P2s above took the shape they did, and left alone they will keep producing more. Worth saying plainly: the token layer is in good shape — contrast ratios are documented inline next to the values, and only 15 hardcoded hex colours survive across all templates. The drift is one level up, in components.

Correction — finding 10 was wrong, and so was the “also” The empty-state count was bad. I grepped for the .empty-state class and called the other 70 templates unhandled. They are not: 69 of 79 use {{range}}…{{else}} or the lighter .empty class. Exactly three templates have a range with no empty handling at all, and all three are right as they are — updates_policy.html's freeze list, for instance, sits directly above its own “+ Add freeze period” button, so empty explains itself. Finding 10 is withdrawn. No shared partial was added; there was nothing to add it for.

The “keyboard-unreachable” line was also wrong. 28 of those ~30 are modal backdrops — click-outside-to-close, with Escape already wired. A backdrop is not a control. Reading the two that were left turned up one genuine trap, below.
Shipped — the one real find in this section The boot-logo splash uploader (/boot-logo) was a <div onclick> opening a display:none file input. The div could not be focused and the input was out of the tab order, so there was no keyboard path to uploading a splash image at all — a complete block on that page's only job, not a papercut. It is now a real <button> with a focus ring. While reading it: the zone said “Drop a splash image” and nothing listened for a drop — no dragover, no drop, anywhere in the file. That is wired now too, into the same input so validation and submit are unchanged.
Shipped as a rule, deliberately not as a migration The winners are frozen in writing: .cc-card, .cc-btn, .cc-tag, a 720 / 1024 / 1280 breakpoint scale, and the heading rule from finding 4 — in a block at the top of style.css where the next person writing a class will hit it, and in the dashboard section of CLAUDE.md. The frozen spellings keep working; nothing new should use them, and a page converts when it is next touched for another reason.

The breakpoints were not rewritten, on purpose. Counting the per-page <style> blocks as well as style.css, there are 22 distinct max-widths, not the 16 reported above — that figure only looked at style.css, so this one was understated rather than overstated. Collapsing 22 live layout rules onto three values changes what happens at specific viewport widths on pages I cannot open in a browser from here. That is a worse risk than the inconsistency it removes, and it is the kind of change that should be made a page at a time by someone looking at the result.
What is left

Findings 1–7 are in. Finding 10 is withdrawn. What these fixes did not settle:

1. Three pages are now reachable that may not deserve to be. /packages, /setup and /setup/managed-configs had no inbound link anywhere. The directory gives them one, which answers “can I find it” and not “should it exist”. Someone who knows the product should say: promote, fold into a neighbour, or delete. 2. The 22 breakpoints. Frozen, not fixed — per page, in a browser. 3. Card-per-row on phones for the device table, once the data-label pass is worth doing properly. 4. Live counts on saved-view pills, after someone measures what another few filtered counts cost on a page that already runs several. 5. The whole live-session half of the audit, which a file read cannot reach at all: perceived latency, WebSocket reconnection, the remote-screen and shell surfaces, and real task timings with real operators. That needs a session recording, not a grep.

On the two corrections. Finding 10 (empty states) was a bad grep — I matched one class name and reported the absence of the pattern rather than the absence of the behaviour. Finding 9 (breakpoints) was undercounted for the same reason: I searched one file when the rules live in two places. Both are recorded above rather than quietly deleted, because an audit that only shows the findings that survived is not one you can calibrate.
