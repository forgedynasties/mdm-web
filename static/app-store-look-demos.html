Store look — what a Play/App Store treatment can and cannot be here

Before the mockups, the constraint that shapes all of them: we have no store artwork. The apps table holds name, icon, version, package, S3 key and added-date. There are no screenshots, no descriptions, no sizes, no changelogs, no ratings — and no analogue for ratings either, because nobody "reviews" a restaurant app. So the look has to be carried by the icon, colour, and fleet facts: how many devices have it, which versions, where it is missing.

What a consumer store sells vs what this one does

Play Store installs to your phone, so its primary button is "Install". Ours installs to a fleet, so the primary is "Install on…" with a count, and the hero metric is not a rating but coverage. Everything else maps cleanly: icon art, shelves, chips, search, and a detail page with version history.

Artwork, if you want the full look

Three ways: (1) generate a gradient per app from its icon's dominant colours — no new data, looks deliberate, what the mockups below use; (2) add an optional banner image per app in the library; (3) screenshots captured from a device running it — we already have a screenshot command, so this is possible, and would be the most store-like thing in the product. (1) now, (3) later, is my suggestion.

Treatment E
Play Store — search, chips, horizontal shelvesmy pick

Pill search, category chips, and shelves you scroll sideways. Big rounded icon art carries it; each card's second line is fleet truth, and the button says what it will do.

Treatment G
The app detail page — where the store look pays off most

Both stores are really about the detail page. Ours has something a consumer store does not: a real answer to "where is this, and what version". Stats where the ratings row would be, version histogram where the screenshots would be, per-device list below.

What I would ship

E plus G: the Play-Store shelves for the index, the detail page for the drill-down, with generated gradient art from each icon's colours so it looks deliberate without inventing data we do not have. F's hero is tempting and I would leave it out of the first pass — it needs a ranking rule and goes empty on a healthy fleet, which is the worst outcome for the most prominent slot on the page.

Two honest trade-offs to accept with E. Sideways shelves hide items, so the chips must include a plain "All apps" grid — an inventory you audit needs a view that shows everything, and that is the layout already on stage. And the shelf "in the library, on no device" is the one I would build first regardless of look: it is computable today, it is where uploads go to die, and nothing in the product currently shows it.

Screenshots are the one piece of real store artwork we could actually have: a screenshot command exists, so a "capture from a device running this" button could fill a detail page with genuine imagery. Worth doing after E and G, not before.
