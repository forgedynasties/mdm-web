Making the Apps page feel finished

Real icons below, pulled from the stage library, so this is what it would actually look like. My first pass was the problem: six rainbow gradient tiles with a letter on them, a coloured button on every card, and three type sizes competing. That reads as a placeholder, because the colour was decoration rather than information.

What made it look cheap

Gradients generated per package meant eight different saturated colours in one row, none of them meaning anything. Every card carried a filled button, so nothing was emphasised. Icons were upscaled. And the card was dense with three lines of text at three sizes.

The principle for the fixes below

Colour is reserved for state — amber means a version we cannot judge, green means it matches the library. Everything else is neutral, and the app's own icon is the only other colour on the page. One action per context, not one per card.

Option 1
Quiet tilesmy pick

Neutral surface tiles with a hairline, the icon at its natural size, one line of name and one of fact. No button per card — the card is the button. Colour appears only as a status pill when something is wrong.

What changes either way

These apply whichever option you pick, and they are most of why the current page looks unfinished:

· Drop the generated gradients. Eight saturated colours per row that mean nothing. Neutral tile, icon at natural size, colour reserved for state.

· One action per context. A filled button on every card means nothing is emphasised; the card itself is the link.

· One line of fact, not two. "on 20 of 20" is the thing; the package name belongs on the detail page.

· Icons at natural size — 60px inside a 104px tile, never scaled past what the file holds.

I would ship option 3: quiet tiles for browsing, rows for the things that need doing. Option 1 alone is the safest and would already look like a different product; option 2 is where this should end up if the library grows past about fifty apps.
