Offline check-ins: keep the readings, show the gap

A plan, 1 Oct 2026, to build after the current work. Today a device that can't reach the MDM (Wi-Fi down, the restaurant's internet out, the server being deployed) records nothing: its battery, temperature and memory for that time are lost, and the device's charts just break the line. The goal: the device keeps its readings in a queue of its own while it can't reach us and sends them when it can, the charts fill in, and the time it was out of contact is shaded so nobody mistakes late data for live data.

How it works

Clientfirmware client 1.7.0

  • When it queues: whenever a check-in fails or the socket is down. While out of contact it takes a reading every 5 minutes (not every 30 s), so a day offline is 288 small entries.
  • What it keeps: the check-in fields the charts use (battery, charging, temperature, RAM, storage, Wi-Fi), plus the device's clock time, the time since boot and the boot id, so the server can place readings correctly even if the device's clock is wrong after a reboot. Crash and reboot events that happened offline go in the same queue.
  • Where: a file in the client's private storage, so it survives a reboot or a client update. Capped at 7 days or 2 MB; when full, the oldest readings go first and the device reports how many it dropped (that is the hatched "no data").
  • Sending: as soon as it can reach the MDM again, oldest first, 200 readings per request to a new POST /api/v1/checkins/backfill with its own key. The server answers with what it stored; the device deletes only that, so a dropped connection mid-upload loses nothing and sends nothing twice.

ServerMDM

  • Stores late readings in the same history (device_samples), marked as late, and ignores any it already has for that moment.
  • Records each out-of-contact span it can see (device_offline_spans: from, to, how many readings arrived late, how many the device dropped). Spans are known on the server even without the new client, so today's gaps get shaded from day one.
  • Late readings never raise alerts (the device was out of contact; it's history now). Late crash events go into the device's history marked "reported late", without paging.
  • Checks the times it's given: nothing in the future, nothing before the device's last live reading, and readings placed by time-since-boot when the device's clock looks wrong.
  • Uptime and "offline" figures keep counting the span as offline: the device couldn't be reached, even though its readings arrived later.

Dashboarddevice page charts

  • All device charts (battery, temperature, RAM and the combined chart) shade the spans, in the option you pick above. Hover says when it was offline and how many readings came late.
  • The CSV export gets a late column.

Decisions for you

  • Shading: A, B or C above. I'd pick C.
  • How long a device keeps readings: 7 days (proposed). Longer costs storage on the device, not the server.
  • Late crashes: history only, no alert (proposed). Say if a crash that happened offline should still page.
Recommendation: C, 7 days, late crashes as history. Step 1 alone already answers "why is there a hole in this chart" for every device today; steps 2 and 3 then fill the holes for devices on 1.7.0.
