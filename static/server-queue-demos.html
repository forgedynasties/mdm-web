The check-in queue on the Server page

Since 4fb4458 a check-in's history goes through an in-process queue: at most 16 check-ins run at once, history is written in batches, and when the queue passes 80% an HTTP check-in is told 429 to come back in 20–60 s. None of that is on the Server page yet — only in /debug/vars. Three ways to show it. Each is drawn twice: a quiet minute, and the burst after a deploy when the fleet reconnects at once. The burst is the only time the numbers mean anything, so judge each option on it.

Nothing on the page Deployed

The queue exists only as JSON under /debug/vars → ingest, behind the admin key. Nobody looking at this page can tell whether devices are being turned away.

Option A
Two rows in Work in flight

A "check-in history queued" bar against the queue's capacity, and a "check-ins turned away" count. Same shape as the rows already there; no collector change.

Good atSmallest change; reads like the rest of the block; the shed count is always visible.
Bad atIt is a snapshot every 2 s of something that lasts seconds. A burst that filled the queue and drained between frames never shows, and after it the bar sits at 0 with no sign anything happened — only the lifetime shed count remembers, and it does not say when.
Option B
A Load tile and a line on the hour Recommended

A "Check-in queue" tile beside Check-ins, showing the peak depth in each 10 s sample rather than the depth at that instant, with batch time and shed count underneath. Like every tile it is also the chart's legend: switch it on and the burst draws against requests and check-ins.

The hourCheck-ins and Check-in queue switched on
Good atThe burst stays on the chart for an hour, lined up with the check-in spike that caused it, so "the deploy at 10:28 brought the fleet back and we turned 40 away for 20 s" is readable after the fact. Peak-per-sample catches a burst the 2 s frame would miss.
Bad atNeeds the collector to keep one more series (the queue reports its peak since the last sample). A seventh tile; on a quiet day it reads 0 and takes a slot in the grid.
Option C
A check-in pipeline card

Its own card under Work in flight: slots in use → queued → written, with shed, written-inline and lost counts beneath. Explains the whole path in one place.

Good atThe most complete picture, and the only one that shows the 16-slot cap filling, which is what turns a check-in away before the queue does.
Bad atMost of the page's height for numbers that are 0 on every normal day at 190 devices, and still a snapshot: the same blindness to a burst that has passed as A.
My pick: B. The queue only does anything for the few seconds after a deploy or a restaurant power cut, so the question this page has to answer is "did that burst hurt, and when" — a trend, not a snapshot. B is the only option that keeps the answer after the burst is over. The shed count rides in the tile's second line, so A's one real strength comes with it.

Mock numbers: quiet = 190 devices checking in normally; burst = the minute after a deploy, when every device reconnects at once (the live deploy on 28 Sep brought 55 back in 2.5 min; this shows the whole fleet).
