AIO Enroll — friendlier, with motion

Same app, same options-A list idea you picked, but three different ways to make it friendly and alive. Each window below is interactive: press ▶ Play to run the whole story — waiting for a device → a phone is plugged in → someone clicks Enroll → seven steps run → done — with a second, blocked device beside it to show the friendly error. All motion respects “reduce motion” in the OS.

Words people see are plain (“Installing the agent…”), never adb output. Errors say what to do, not what failed.

Option A
Calm list with live progress recommended
Keeps the list you chose. Waiting state is a soft radar pulse. A device slides in; on Enroll the row grows a progress bar with the current step written in words; on success the row flashes green and the check draws itself. Blocked rows explain the fix right on the row.

Good: least change from what you approved, scales to many devices, everything visible at once. Bad: less “wow” than the others; progress is a thin bar.

Option B
Focus panel with a step timeline
Left rail lists devices with a status dot; the big panel shows the selected one. Pick a class as pills, press a large Enroll button, and a vertical timeline fills in step by step with a ring burst and big check at the end. Most guided, great for a first-timer.

Good: impossible to miss what is happening; feels like a setup assistant. Bad: one device in focus at a time, so batch enrolling means extra clicks.

Option C
Bench tiles with progress rings and a counter
Each device is a tile with a ring that fills as it enrolls, then turns green with a small confetti pop. A running “enrolled today” counter bumps on every success — good motivation on a bench doing 30 devices. Blocked tiles shake once and show the fix.

Good: several devices enrolling in parallel are easy to watch; fun. Bad: least information per device; confetti may feel unserious to some.

Recommendation

A, plus two bits borrowed: B's step wording as a tooltip-style detail, and C's “enrolled today” counter in the footer. It keeps batch work fast and still feels polished.

Also planned in all options: light/dark following the OS, a first-run welcome that says “Plug in a device”, a one-line “adb not found / driver missing” help card, and Esc/Enter keyboard shortcuts.
