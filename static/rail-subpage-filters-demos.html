A sub-page and its filters in one rail

On /jobs/history the rail has to say two things at once: you are on History, which is a page, and you are looking at Everything, which is a filter of that page. Lighting both reads as two selections; lighting only the filter loses the page. Five ways to tell the two levels apart — the body in each mock shows History with its "Everything" filter on.

Today on stage

Both rows in the same flat list, only the filter lit — so "History" looks unvisited even though you are on it.

A · The page becomes the heading its filters sit under my pick

The rail still lists the section's pages. The one you are on stops being a row and becomes the heading of its own filter list, nested under a hairline. One fill in the rail — the active filter — and the page is stated, not competed with. Good: the hierarchy is visible, it scales to any page with filters, and nothing is lit twice. Bad: the current page is no longer a clickable row, so "show me History unfiltered" has to be the first filter ("Everything"), which it already is.

B · The rail's title is the breadcrumb

On a sub-page the rail re-titles itself Jobs › History, offers one way back up, and lists only the filters. Good: the least furniture of any option, and the title area was doing nothing anyway. Bad: the section's other pages disappear while you are in a sub-page, so moving from History to In flight is two steps.

C · Two weights: a bar for the page, a fill for the filter

Everything stays in one flat list, but the two levels are drawn differently — the page you are on carries an accent bar on its edge, the active filter keeps the soft fill. Good: smallest change to what ships today, and both rows stay clickable. Bad: it asks people to learn that a bar and a fill mean different things; at a glance it is still two marked rows.

D · Filters are not navigation — put them on the page

The rail holds pages only; the filter becomes a segmented control at the top of the content, where the thing it filters is. Good: the cleanest separation — the rail answers "where", the page answers "what of it" — and it matches how filters already work on Fleet and Apps. Bad: the filter leaves the rail, so the count per state is no longer visible from the nav, and the control scrolls away with the page unless pinned.

E · The filter is a control in the rail, not a list of rows

One row per page, and under the current page a single select showing the filter in force. Good: shortest rail of the five, and it reads unambiguously — a control is clearly not a destination. Bad: hides the other states and their counts behind a click, which is exactly what a four-item filter should not need.

Pick: A, with D as the alternative. A keeps both levels visible and lights exactly one thing, and the nesting is a pattern that works for every page that grows filters later — Alerts, Clients, Fleet.
