Temperature chart — fast readings

Firmware v2.1.023 smooths the battery temperature in healthd, and client 1.5.0 can send it every time it moves 0.2 °C (at most every 10 s, at least every 60 s) while the MDM has fast temperature switched on for that device. Every such reading is kept for 24 h. These are four ways to show it on the device page. The data is a mock of a T7 warming up on the charger and cooling after; the same six hours feed every option.

Option 1
One point a minute deployed today
The Temp tab as it is: device_samples, at most one row a minute. The client only sent a new temperature when it moved 1 °C, so the stored rows repeat the last value sent and the line climbs in steps.
Good: nothing to build. Bad: up to 0.9 °C behind the device at any moment; a charger warm-up looks like a staircase, so the rate of rise is unreadable.

Device graphs

Option 2
Same Temp tab, every reading for the last 24 h recommended
When the device has fast readings, the Temp tab plots those for the last 24 h and falls back to the one-a-minute history before that. A small note beside the tabs says the line is every reading and how old the newest one is; the latest value is labelled at the end of the line.
Good: no new place to look — the chart people already use simply becomes live and exact; devices without fast mode look exactly as today. Bad: nothing on the page tells you fast mode exists until it is on for that device.

Option 3
Live temperature card beside the graphs
A separate card: the current reading as a large number with its age, the last hour's min and max, and a one-hour sparkline. The Temp tab keeps the one-a-minute history.
Good: the live number is readable at a glance from across the room. Bad: two temperature views that disagree about the past (the tab still steps); one more card on a long page.

Option 4
Readings view — every reading as a point, with a table
Option 2's chart with each reading drawn as a dot, and a table of readings underneath (time, °C, change, device uptime) with a CSV download. Admin-only; meant for validation against adb.
Good: shows the cadence and every value exactly — the table is also the accessible view of the chart. Bad: noisy for an operator who only wants to know if the tablet is hot.

Pick: Option 2. The question people bring to the device page is "how hot is it, and is it rising?" — the Temp tab already answers that, and with every reading it answers it correctly and live. It needs no new layout, and devices without fast mode are unchanged. Option 4's table is worth adding later as an admin toggle on the same tab, rather than as its own card, when fast mode leaves testing.
