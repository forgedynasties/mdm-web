Selection panel — five ways to hold 14 actions

Tick some devices on Fleet and a panel appears with fourteen buttons in four labelled groups, all at equal weight. Every option below is mocked over a slice of the real roster, in the real theme — the panels open, the menus work, the tabs switch.

The count that matters: Send 4 · Organize 4 (5 inside a group) · Data 4 · Admin 3. Two of them — Action and Push update — are the ones that carry real weight; the rest are shortcuts, two of which (Reboot, Screenshot) just pre-fill the same Actions composer that Action opens. Three are destructive (Retire, Mark inactive, Remove from group) and currently sit in the same visual language as Copy serials.
Option 0 · what is deployed
The sheetToday

A right-edge panel, four labelled sections, every action a same-size button in a 2-up grid. Nothing is hidden and nothing is ranked: Action and Mark inactive are one tab-stop apart and the same size.

Good at
  • Everything is visible — no menu to learn, no second click.
  • The grouping is genuinely good: Send / Organize / Data / Admin is the right taxonomy.
  • Fixed to the right edge, so the roster stays readable behind it.
Bad at
  • No hierarchy. The one button that matters and the one that retires devices look identical.
  • Destructive actions sit in the grid like everything else.
  • Scrolls on a laptop — Admin is below the fold, which is the worst place for Retire to hide.
  • Adding a 15th action makes it worse in a way nothing here bounds.

Good at
  • States what the panel is for. One primary, a few shortcuts, everything else one click away.
  • Destructive actions live behind a divider at the bottom of a menu — where they belong.
  • Fits without scrolling at any height, and a 15th action costs one menu row.
  • Smallest change from what is deployed: same buttons, regrouped.
Bad at
  • The nine in the menu become one click slower, every time.
  • Someone has to decide which four are promoted, and that decision will be wrong for somebody.
  • Discovery suffers: a new operator no longer sees the whole surface at a glance.
Option B
Bottom bar, not a side sheet

The pattern every table with bulk actions eventually lands on: a compact bar that rises from the bottom edge when a selection exists. Horizontal, so it holds far less — which forces the ranking rather than inviting the grid.

6 visible · rest in ⋯
Good at
  • Gives the roster its full width back — the side sheet costs 300px of table.
  • Familiar: this is how bulk selection works nearly everywhere else.
  • The shape itself limits the button count, so it cannot rot the way a grid can.
Bad at
  • Collides with the nav dock, which already floats bottom-centre. That is a real layout fight, not a detail.
  • Holds the fewest actions of any option — almost everything ends up in the overflow.
  • No room for the selection summary beyond a count.
Option C
Same sheet, tabbed

Keep the sheet and keep all fourteen, but show one group at a time. Send is the default; Organize, Data and Admin are a tab away. Nothing is removed, nothing scrolls, and Admin stops being a thing you find by accident while scrolling past it.

Option D
Verb list, searchable

Drop the grid. One column of rows, each an icon, a verb and a chevron where it opens something. A filter box at the top because a list you can type into does not care how long it gets — which is the one property none of the other options have.

What I would do
A. The panel's problem is not that fourteen actions exist — it is that all fourteen are shouting equally. Action opens the composer that can do any of them; Reboot and Screenshot are literally that same composer pre-filled. So the panel is already mostly one button wearing four hats, and A is just admitting that.

The part I would not compromise on is the destructive three. Retire, Mark inactive and Remove from group are currently the same size, colour and weight as Copy serials, and on a laptop Admin is below the fold — so the most dangerous group is also the least visible. In A they sit at the bottom of the menu behind a divider, which is where every other product puts them and where this one should too.

C is the safe answer and worth taking if the objection to A is "do not hide anything from operators". It keeps all fourteen first-class and still stops the scrolling. It just does not fix the ranking, which I think is the actual complaint.

Not B. The dock already floats bottom-centre; putting a second floating bar there is a fight over the same 60px of screen, and one of them would have to move.

D is the right answer to a problem you do not have yet. If bulk actions grow past twenty, revisit it — the filter box is the only mechanism here that genuinely does not care how long the list gets.

All five are live: A's menu opens, C's tabs switch, D filters as you type. They use style.css tokens, so the theme switch top-right renders each in both themes. The device rows behind them are mock data.
Separately: the duplicate-panel bug in your screenshot is fixed and shipped — the sheet lives under <body> and survives a roster refresh, but that refresh renders a second #bulk-bar, and both were ending up inside it. Nothing else here is wired to the dashboard.
