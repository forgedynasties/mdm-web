AIO Enroll — a calmer left rail

Right now every phone found on the network gets its own big orange card with a loud Connect button. With five phones around, they crowd out your real devices and all look identical (the network only tells us an address — no model — until a phone is paired). Three ways to calm it down:

Today (too loud)
Problems: 1) five identical full-width cards, 2) a primary-colour button on each, 3) they push “Add over Wi-Fi” and Saved phones off screen, 4) shown permanently even if you never use Wi-Fi.
Option A
Collapsed “Nearby phones (5)” group recommended
One quiet row with a count badge. Closed by default, so the rail stays about your devices. Click to expand into slim one-line rows; the Connect button only appears on hover/focus. The badge pops when a new phone appears, so you still notice.
Pick a device on the left.
Click Nearby phones to expand it.

Good: smallest change, rail stays calm, nothing is hidden for good. Bad: one extra click to see addresses.

Option B
One summary row; the list lives in the main panel
The rail only shows “5 phones nearby ›”. Clicking opens a roomy list in the big panel with larger rows, a spinner per Connect, and results inline (connected ✓, or “needs pairing”). Rail stays tiny however many phones are around.
Pick a device on the left.
Click Nearby phones to open the list here.

Good: scales to dozens of phones, room for status and later for model names. Bad: takes over the main panel while open.

Option C
Compact scrolling box with search and “show more”
Always visible but boxed: shows the first 3 with the rest behind “Show 2 more”, a small filter field appears once there are more than 3, and the list never grows past a fixed height.
Pick a device on the left.

Good: always in view, searchable. Bad: still takes rail space all the time.

Extra cleanups (any option)

• Quiet button: outline “Connect” instead of solid orange (orange stays for the one thing you should do next).
• Hide unrelated phones: nearby phones the user has never touched could be tucked behind “Saved first, others on request”.
• Names: a nearby phone gets a real name as soon as it is paired once (we remember it), so “Phone on your network” turns into “Pixel 6”.
• “Add over Wi-Fi” becomes a small ghost button, not a big dashed orange block.
• A setting to turn discovery off for people who only use USB.

Pick A, B or C (they can combine, e.g. A for the rail and B's panel when you press “Add over Wi-Fi”).
