Device page: graph in one pane, tabs in the other

Today the graph is on Overview only, so switching to Applications or Activity loses it. A split keeps the Vitals graph in view while the other pane changes tab, so you can watch the battery or temperature while you install an app, read the queue or check an alert. The top bar and the facts line stay as they are above both panes. On a phone the panes stack (graph first). Device: the T7 from your screenshot.

S1 · Graph left, tabs right my pick

The left pane is the Vitals graph and stays put (sticky while the right pane scrolls). The right pane has the tabs; Overview there no longer repeats the graph. The divider can be dragged to give either side more room, and a double-click on it resets to 55 / 45.

Good: you read left to right: how the device is doing, then what to do about it; the graph is never more than a glance away on any tab; lists in the right pane are a comfortable reading width.
Bad: each pane is narrower than today's full width, so wide tables (Activity, Applications) show fewer columns; below about 1100px it stacks.

S2 · Tabs left, graph right

The same split mirrored: the working pane first, the graph as a narrower, taller monitor on the right.

Good: the tabs keep the place your eye goes first; the graph reads as a side monitor.
Bad: the graph is narrow (about 40%), so 24h and 7d views get cramped; the island's buttons sit above the wrong side.

S3 · Graph pinned on top, tabs below

Not side by side: the graph becomes a compact band across the full width (all three charts, shorter) that stays visible above every tab, and can be collapsed to a one-line sparkline strip.

Good: the graph keeps the full width (best for long ranges); the tabs get full width too.
Bad: the band takes vertical space on every tab; on a laptop screen the tab content starts lower.

S4 · Split on demand

Today's layout by default (graph on Overview only), plus a "Split" button on the graph. Pressing it docks the graph as a left pane like S1 and keeps it there on every tab until you undock it; the choice is remembered per person.

Good: nobody loses width they did not ask to give up; people who watch devices live get the split.
Bad: two layouts to keep working; a mode people have to discover.

My recommendation

S1. The point of the split is keeping the graph in view while you act on the device, and putting it first (left) matches how the page reads: state, then actions. A draggable divider covers the cases where a tab needs more room.
