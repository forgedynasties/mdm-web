The child rail while the app rail is open

Hovering the app rail expands it to 196px and squeezes the page's own rail into 56px. The squeeze is what forced two-letter initials — Al Ne In Co — and they are the problem: they name nothing, they repeat (Al is both "All" and "Alerts"), and they look like tags. Every option below is a different answer to what occupies those 56px, or whether the rail should be squeezed at all. The state shown in each mock is mid-hover — pointer on the app rail — because at rest the child rail is already full width with real labels.

A · Two-letter initials on stage now — the thing you don't like

Shown for comparison. Bad: abbreviations of arbitrary labels, ambiguous and unreadable at a glance.

B · An icon per entry

Each child row carries a glyph, exactly as the app rail's rows do, and the collapsed strip is that column of glyphs. Fixed rails (Settings, Alerts, History) get a hand-picked icon per entry; the generated ones (a venue, a group, a build) get the icon of their kind — pin for a venue, people for a group, download for a build — not of the individual row. Good: one visual language with the app rail, nothing to decode, scales to any label. Bad: two venues look identical collapsed; it tells you the kind, not the which.

C · Colour swatch per entry

No letters at all: each row keeps a stable colour derived from its name — the same trick the Apps store art uses — and the active one gets a ring. Good: genuinely distinguishes two venues from each other, which icons cannot. Bad: a colour means nothing until you have used the page a few times, and it collides with the status colours (green/amber/red) already in that rail.

D · Don't squeeze it — the app rail floats over the page my pick

The child rail never collapses and never changes: full width, real labels, counts, the lot. Hovering the app rail expands it over the content with a shadow and a faint scrim, the way a temporary drawer does, and it is gone the moment you leave. Good: deletes the problem rather than dressing it — nothing to abbreviate, nothing to learn, and the page's own nav stays usable while you aim at the app rail. Bad: the overlay covers ~140px of the content for as long as you hover, so something you were reading is briefly hidden. That was the original reason for the reciprocal swap — but a swap that makes the rail unreadable costs more than a shadow that passes.

E · Position ticks

The strip stops pretending to be readable: one short dash per entry, the current one longer and accented. It is a scrollbar-grade hint — "four things, you are on the first". Good: honest, silent, no vocabulary, no collision with anything. Bad: carries the least information of any option; you can count, not choose.

F · It slides away, leaving a seam

The child rail goes off-canvas while the app rail is open; a 10px seam with a grip holds its place, and it slides back when you leave. Good: no half-state to design at all, and the swap stays width-neutral (196 + 10 ≈ 56 + 150, content shifts by a hair). Bad: the page's own nav disappearing on the way to the app nav is exactly the complaint that started this; a seam is thinner than the 56px strip, so it is a weaker promise that it comes back.

Pick: D, with B as the fallback. Every lettered or abbreviated strip is an attempt to compress labels that refuse to compress — venues and groups are user-typed names, so there is no abbreviation scheme that works for all of them. Floating the app rail removes the compression entirely and costs a shadow passing over the content for a second. If the overlay is not wanted, B is the only compressed form that stays readable, because it switches from text to the icon vocabulary the app rail already uses.
