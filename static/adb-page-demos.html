Wireless adb — its own page, and connecting from anywhere

Two things to decide. 1 · A page listing every device wireless adb was ever pushed to (the adb_tcp history), where it can be turned on and off in batch. 2 · Reach: today the address shown is the device's LAN IP, useless from outside the restaurant. The proposal is a tunnel through the MDM server (agent relays 127.0.0.1:5555 over a WebSocket; you run a small local bridge and adb connect 127.0.0.1:PORT). Admin only, same as the command today.

Today Deployed

One switch per device, on the device page (Security card + Settings → Wireless adb). No list of which devices have adb open, no batch, and ValidateAdbTcp refuses anything but one named device. The address is LAN-only.

1 · The page (/adb, "More → Control → Wireless adb", admin only)

Who is on it: every device with at least one adb_tcp command in its history, plus a filter to show every device that could take one (firmware client 1.4.9+ on v2.1.022+, or a standard-client device whose adbd already listens on 5555). Batch = the server fans one adb_tcp per named device, so the "never a group, never all" rule still holds — you tick serials, not a venue.

A · Ledger — one table, tick rows, act on them Recommended

Good: same shape as Devices and Updates, so nothing to learn; batch is tick → bar; the row says state, reach, who turned it on and when, and why the last try failed. The connect line opens under the row, so one page does on/off and connect. Bad: flat — with 40 open devices across 10 venues you lean on the venue filter.

2 · Where the connect-from-anywhere control lives

Independent of the page layout. The tunnel is a session: admin opens it, it dies after 60 min idle or on End, and the activity log records it. The bridge is a tiny CLI (mdm-adb, Go, in cmd/) signed in with your dashboard account — later also a button in AIO Enroll. No public ports on the server; a direct adb connect mdm.dev.aioapp.com:4xxxx was rejected because an HK1-class box with ro.adb.secure=0 would be a root shell to anyone who guessed the port.

I · Row drawer (as in A) Recommended

"Connect ▾" on the row expands the drawer: tunnel command, LAN command, session state, End. Nothing leaves the list.

II · Sessions card at the top of the page Chosen

A "Live tunnels" card above the table listing every open session (device, who, time left, End). Rows only get "Connect". Good: one glance shows who is inside what. Bad: two places to look for one device; fine to add later on top of I.
