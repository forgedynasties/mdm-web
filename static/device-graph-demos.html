Device page: the Vitals graph

Now that the graph runs the full width, the card around it shows its age: three rows of controls (time zone, ranges, shift, markers, five tool icons), then metric tabs, two legends and a side panel that holds the hovered values. Three ways to make it read like one instrument. Data is a made-up but realistic day for a T7 at a restaurant: overnight charge, service from 11:00, a guest phone on and off the pad. A kiosk or other mains device shows the same thing without the battery.

Now on stage

Every control is visible all the time; one metric at a time; values for the hovered moment appear in the column on the right.

G1 · Stacked, one shared timeline my pick

Battery, Temp and RAM are drawn one above another on the same time axis, so a hover is one vertical line through all three, and each row’s big number on the left changes to the value at that moment. The header keeps only the range and ‹ ›; time zone, the marker toggles, PNG and full screen go in ⋯. Command and build markers run through all rows as thin lines.

G2 · Metric tiles above one chart

The tabs become tiles: each shows its current value, a short note and a sparkline of the range. Click a tile to draw it big below. Hover values appear in a tooltip at the cursor, not a side column.

My recommendation

G1. The question people bring to this graph is "what happened to this device and when", and the answer is almost always a relationship (hot because charging and busy, RAM climbing until a reboot). A shared timeline answers it at a glance, and folding the rarely used controls into ⋯ is what makes room for it.
