Graph readout & battery cycles

Two things in one page. Part 1 — the strip under the graph: it should read one check-in, the nearest one to the pointer, and it should look like something. Part 2 — showing cycles on the graph, where a cycle means charged to about full, then run down to about empty, not our lifetime discharge÷100 estimate. Graphs are static mocks; the numbers are made up but shaped like a real T7 day.

Part 1 · The hover readout

Today
One long sentence in a footer bar deployed

Each metric is looked up independently — nearest battery point, nearest temp point, nearest RAM point — so the line can mix three different check-ins and still print one timestamp. Values run together in prose, and the row is empty-looking when a metric has no reading nearby.

Battery86%Temp55.5°CRAM26.9%All

Good at Cheap, one line, never moves.
Bad at Mixes check-ins; prose runs together; no units alignment; battery silently absent; looks like a status bar, not a readout.
Option A
One check-in, labelled columns my pick

The strip stays where it is but reads one check-in — the nearest to the pointer — and prints its values as labelled columns with units. The time carries how far the pointer is from it (±12s), so a sparse window is honest instead of silently snapping. A metric the device did not report in that check-in shows "—".

Battery86%Temp55.5°CRAM26.9%All
48h7d30d
Sep 21, 09:14:07±12s Battery86% Charge⚡ pad Temp55.5°C RAM26.9% Last 48 hours
Good at Exactly what was asked: one check-in, its own values. Columns align as you sweep, so numbers stop jumping around. No new page furniture.
Bad at Still a bar at the bottom — your eye travels from the cursor to it. Four columns is about the limit before it wraps on a laptop.

The readout follows the pointer as a small card next to the point, coloured per series, with the check-in's time as its heading. The footer shrinks back to the window label.

Part 2 · Cycles on the graph

A cycle here is a real excursion: the pack reaches about full, then is run down to about empty. Two thresholds and a rule, all tunable:

Full mark≥ 95% (a device that tops out at 97% still counts)
Empty mark≤ 10%
Counted whenthe pack crosses the full mark and later crosses the empty mark — the cycle lands at the empty crossing
Partialfull → 35% → charged again = not a cycle; shown as a faint arc, not counted

This is a different number from the Battery cycles in About, which is lifetime discharge ÷ 100 (every 1% drop, added up — "equivalent full cycles"). The two will not agree and should not: one counts real full-depth excursions, the other totals wear. Both are worth having, and the demos below label them separately.

Option 1
Cycle ticks under the battery line my pick

A small mark on the axis at every completed cycle, with a count chip in the footer. The graph gains four marks a week, not a new lane.

One thing to decide with it: no device reports a cycle count today — I checked every telemetry field on stage, there is no cycle_count. So these counts are computed server-side from the battery history we already store, which means they work retroactively on existing data and cost nothing on the device. If you would rather have the pack's own counter (what the fuel gauge reports), that is a firmware-client change first — and it would be a third number next to these two.

My picks: A + 1. A does what you asked — one check-in, its own values, in columns that do not jump — without moving the readout away from where it lives now. 1 adds cycles as a handful of marks plus a count for the window on screen, and leaves the plot alone. If you want the readout to stop being a bar at all, B is the one to pick, and it pairs fine with 1.
