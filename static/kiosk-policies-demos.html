Policies: kiosk only

You asked for the Policies page to be kiosk and nothing else, with a proper look at how kiosk policies work. Part 1 is the audit of the current page and its logic. Parts 2 and 3 are options for the page and for creating a policy. Part 4 is what changes behind them. Restaurant names, device counts and app names are from live on 30 Sep: Flights Vegas has 20 tablets running Nugget-uatv2 and Nugget-stagev2, Huanchaco Peruvian has 8 running Nugget. Today there are no kiosk policies and no device is kiosk-locked, so nothing on live changes behaviour until a policy is made.

Part 1

Audit of the current kiosk policies

SeverityWhat's wrongWhere
HighIt isn't enforced. The page says "apply once, keep enforcing", but a policy writes kiosk settings to the devices in its target at the moment it is saved, and never again. A tablet added to Flights Vegas next week is not locked; one moved out stays locked.Logic
HighPolicies and the device page fight silently. Both write the same kiosk setting on the device; whichever saved last wins. The device page doesn't say a policy manages it, and turning kiosk off there quietly breaks the policy.Logic, device page
HighIt can lock to an app the device doesn't have. The policy picks from every app seen anywhere in the fleet. The device page refuses this for a reason: lock-task silently fails when the app isn't installed. The policy page gives no warning.Editor
HighOverlaps and deletes. A device in two policies gets whichever was saved last, with no warning. "Duplicate" creates an instant overlap. Deleting a policy unlocks every device in it, even ones another policy still covers.Logic
MediumNo result. After saving you see a device count, not whether the devices actually locked. There's no "locked / waiting / can't lock / app missing" per policy, and no list of the devices.List
MediumNumbers that don't add up. "Covers X of Y devices" adds up each policy's targets, so overlapping policies are counted twice. "Unclaimed" means "kiosk is off", not "no policy targets it".Header
MediumMissing what kiosk can do on a device. The offline exit code (a technician leaves kiosk on site without the server) exists per device but not in a policy.Editor
MediumFive tabs that aren't kiosk: Updates, Network, Managed configs, Compliance and Geofencing. You asked for them to go. The fleet GPS switch and the compliance/geofencing pages go with them.Tabs
LowThe editor is three separate cards (name, targets, app) with the name first, so you name a policy before knowing what it does. The device picker lists every device in the fleet. The "Everything else — Default" card doesn't let you do anything.Editor, list

A · A rules list, top rule wins Recommended

No tabs. Each policy is one row: its app, where it applies, and what actually happened on the devices (locked, waiting for the device, can't lock, app missing). Rules are in order; a device covered by two uses the higher one, and the row says so. The last row is what happens to everything else, and it can be changed. Part 4

Behaviour behind either design

Enforced, not applied onceThe server works out each device's kiosk setting from the rules whenever a device checks in or moves restaurant/group, so new devices lock and moved ones unlock.
Order decides overlapsA device gets the highest rule that covers it. Deleting a rule falls back to the next one (or "everything else"), never to unlocked by accident.
Device page knowsKiosk on a device says "Locked by Flights Vegas · guest tablets". Changing it there asks whether to override this device (an exception that stays) or edit the rule.
App must be installedA device without the app shows "app missing" on the rule, isn't told to lock, and locks by itself once the app is installed.
Exit codes per ruleThe offline exit code and re-lock time are set on the rule and apply to its devices.
Only kioskThe Updates, Network, Managed configs, Compliance and Geofencing tabs and their pages are removed from the dashboard (the update behaviour already lives with releases). Their data stays in the database, so nothing is lost if one is wanted back.
Device pageKiosk onLocked to Nugget-uatv2 by rule Flights Vegas · guest tabletsOverride on this device

Recommendation

A, the rules list, with E1, the three-question editor, and all of Part 4. A is the only option that shows whether devices actually locked and makes overlaps predictable. E1 is the only one that can't lock to a missing app. The Part 4 changes are what make "policy" mean something: without continuous enforcement and the device page knowing about rules, any design shows numbers that stop being true a day later.
