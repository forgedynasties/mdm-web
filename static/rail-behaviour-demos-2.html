Rail behaviour — second round

All six are live: move the pointer into each mock and see what the rail does. What is on stage today is M — hover opens, and every arrival flashes it open for a beat. The rest are different opinions about when a navigation rail has earned the right to move. The badge in the corner of each mock says what that one is thinking.

M · Hover + flash on arrival on stage now

Crossing the rail opens it after 120ms; leaving folds it after 220ms; every page you land on flashes it open for 1.1s. Bad: it opens whenever the pointer so much as travels past the left edge on its way somewhere else, which on a wide screen is often.

N · Intent, not proximity my pick

It opens only when the pointer is aiming at it: moving leftward, slowly, and still inside after ~90ms. A fast diagonal across the rail on the way to a table leaves it shut. Same close as today. Good: kills the single most annoying thing about hover rails — the one that opens when you did not ask — without costing a click when you did. Bad: "sometimes it opens, sometimes it does not" if the thresholds are wrong; it needs tuning against a real hand, not a spec.

↓ try swiping across quickly, then approaching slowly

O · Hot edge — a 7px strip, not the whole rail

The icons do nothing on hover; the first 7px of the screen does. Arriving from the far edge is a gesture you make on purpose, and nothing else lives there. Good: dead simple to explain and impossible to trigger by accident; the icon column stays a quiet row of targets you can click straight through. Bad: a 7px target is small on a 4K screen, and the affordance is invisible until someone finds it by accident.

↓ touch the very left edge

Q · Reading mode — it folds when you start working

The rail opens on hover as usual, but it also folds itself the moment you scroll the content or type, and does not come back until the pointer returns to the left edge. Good: matches intent rather than position — someone scrolling a device table has finished navigating — and gives long tables their width back automatically. Bad: state that changes from something you did elsewhere can feel haunted the first time it happens.

↓ hover to open, then scroll inside the page area

R · Keyboard-first, mouse-second

⌘K opens the palette, ⌘1…⌘9 jump straight to a rail row, and the rail itself opens only on focus or on a click of the logo — hover does nothing. The folded numbers are the shortcuts, so the rail teaches itself. Good: for the person who lives in this dashboard all day, this is the fastest the nav can be, and nothing ever moves unasked. Bad: discovery is a cliff; a monitoring screen that nobody types at gets a nav that never opens.

↓ click inside, then press 1–5 (⌘ optional here)

S · Sticky by page — it remembers per section

Open the rail on Fleet and it stays open on Fleet, for good. Fold it on Apps and Apps stays folded. Each page remembers how you last left it, per browser. Good: the pages where you navigate constantly settle open, the ones you read settle shut, and nobody has to choose a global policy. Bad: the same rail behaving differently on two pages is hard to explain, and the setting is invisible — you only find out by noticing.

↓ fold/unfold with the logo, then hop pages and come back

Pick: N, with Q as a second layer. Intent-based opening fixes the real complaint about hover rails while keeping a rail you never have to click; reading mode is the one rule that reliably gets the width back when you need it. R is the one to build if you want the dashboard to feel like a tool rather than a site — and the ⌘1…⌘9 part is worth stealing into whichever option wins.
