The rail: logo, icons, no header — and the Fleet page's second rail

Four things from the screenshot. The frozen highlight and the leftover "New kiosk rule" buttons were plain bugs and are already fixed on stage. These are the design calls: where the logo and the account controls go once the header is gone, and what happens on Fleet, which has a rail of its own.

Good at One piece of chrome instead of two, and the rail earns its width by holding everything the header did. Icons make the groups scannable without reading. The brand sits where a product's brand normally sits.
Bad at The account menu and theme toggle are further from where people expect them (top-right). On short screens the rail scrolls, and the account can fall below the fold unless it is pinned — which is what the footer does here.
2 · Fleet has a rail of its own

The Fleet page carries a collections rail — restaurants and groups — so with the app rail there are two left rails against each other. Three ways out.

A — collections move to the rightmy pick

Navigation on the left, filtering on the right. They stop competing because they are different kinds of thing: one moves you between pages, the other narrows what is on this one.

3 · What I would ship
Recommendation Rail as in §1 — logo top, icons throughout, account pinned at the bottom, header gone — and A on Fleet, with the collections rail moving to the right. Left moves you between pages, right narrows the page you are on; that rule is easy to hold and survives the next page that wants a filter rail. If the 700px list turns out too tight in use, B is the fallback and the chips already exist on Apps to copy.
