The dock: nine other ideas

The dock today is a floating glass pill with Overview, Fleet, Actions and Alerts, then your three most-visited pages, then More, which opens the page search. Below are nine other ways it could work. Some are small changes to the same pill and some replace it. Every mock is interactive: hover, click, and watch. Numbers are sample data from a fleet like live (151 devices, 95 offline, 2 alerts).

Current: glass pill Deployed

Four fixed destinations, your three most-visited pages (quieter, after the divider), and More.

B · Live dock: every item says how it's doing Recommended

The same pill, but each destination carries its live state. Fleet shows a small online ring ("56 / 151"), Alerts a red count that pulses while something is critical, and Actions a spinner while commands are still running. You can tell whether a page needs you before you open it.

Good at
  • The smallest change with the biggest payoff: same layout and muscle memory, and the dock stops being decoration.
  • Uses numbers the server already sends: the alert count in the top bar, the online count, and commands in flight.
Bad at
  • A permanently pulsing badge gets tuned out. It needs to pulse only while something is critical, not merely open.

C · Magnify: icons only, grows under the cursor Surprise

The macOS dock: square icon tiles that swell as the cursor passes over them, with the name in a tooltip. The dock takes up less than half the width.

Move the cursor slowly along it.

Good at
  • Takes very little space and feels playful.
  • Looks the same on a phone and a desktop.
Bad at
  • No labels. New staff have to hover to learn what the lightning bolt means.
  • Motion in a work tool is fun for a week.

D · Search bar dock

The dock is the search. Four icon buttons on the left, and a wide field on the right that takes a serial, a restaurant, a page or an action ("reboot T7-0412"). Click the field to see suggestions. This merges the dock and the ⌘K palette into one thing at the bottom of the screen.

Click the search field.

Good at
  • Most visits are "go to this device". Typing a serial is faster than Fleet → search → click.
  • It makes the palette easy to find. Today only people who know ⌘K use it.
Bad at
  • The page names shrink to icons.
  • It duplicates the search box in the top bar, so one of the two should go.

E · Orb: one button, everything fans out Surprise

No dock at all, just one brand-gradient button in the corner with the alert count on it. Click it and the destinations fan out in an arc. The bottom of every page is free for content.

Click the orb.

Good at
  • Frees the whole screen. Tables and the device wall stop hiding behind the dock.
  • Memorable, and a nice fit with the brand gradient.
Bad at
  • Every page change is now two clicks instead of one.
  • You can't see where you are from the navigation.

F · Edge rail

A slim icon rail on the left edge that widens on hover to show names and groups (Fleet, Operate, Admin). This is the Linear / Vercel / Stripe pattern. Everything in the "More" directory can live here, so nothing needs the palette.

Hover the rail.

Good at
  • Room for every page, grouped. It ends the "page with no inbound link" problem for good.
  • The most familiar pattern for admin consoles.
Bad at
  • Takes 56px of width on every page, which is a lot on a phone. It needs its own mobile treatment.
  • The dock was a deliberate move away from a sidebar.

G · Page-aware dock

The dock changes with the page. The main navigation shrinks to a home button and a breadcrumb, and the rest of the dock becomes the page's own actions: Screenshot / Reboot / Shell on a device, Send to selection / Export on Fleet. The navigation lives in the top bar and the palette.

Switch pages with the buttons at the top right.

Good at
  • The things you do on a page are always under your thumb, even while scrolling a long device page.
Bad at
  • A dock that changes all the time can't be learned. It fights with the pages' own buttons.
  • It's the biggest change: every page has to declare its actions.

H · Peek: hover to see the page before you open it

The same pill. Hovering an item pops a small card with that page's key numbers: Fleet's online and offline counts, Alerts' newest critical, Actions' last command. Often the card is enough, and you don't have to go there.

Hover Fleet, Actions or Alerts.

Good at
  • Answers "anything new?" without a page change.
  • Pairs well with B: the badge says something changed, the peek says what.
Bad at
  • No hover on touch screens, so a phone never sees it.
  • Every hover is another request, so it needs a short cache.

I · Island: the dock turns into the news Surprise

Like the iPhone's Dynamic Island. When something happens (a restaurant goes offline, a rollout finishes), the dark dock morphs into a notification for a few seconds with a button to go there, then shrinks back into the navigation. It replaces toasts.

Watch it for five seconds, or click the dock to trigger an event.

Good at
  • News lands where your eyes already go, and the animation makes it impossible to miss.
  • Uses the live event stream the dashboard already has.
Bad at
  • A busy fleet would keep the dock in notification mode, so events need throttling to critical-only.
  • Always dark, whatever the theme.

J · Status bar: flat, full width, with live counts

No floating pill. A slim bar pinned to the bottom edge, like VS Code's: tabs on the left, with live fleet counts and the ⌘K hint on the right. Quiet, dense, and it never covers content.

Good at
  • Never covers a table row or the last card on a page.
  • Room for live status next to the navigation.
Bad at
  • The least distinctive. It gives up the dock's character.

Recommendation: B, and H later

B, the live dock. It keeps what works today (four fixed destinations, your frequent pages, More) and makes the dock report on the fleet: online ring on Fleet, a count on Alerts that pulses only while something is critical, a spinner on Actions while commands run. It's the smallest change that makes the dock useful, and all the numbers already exist. H (peek) fits on top of it later: the badge says something changed, the peek says what. I (island) is the fun one if you want the dock to become the news. Pick a letter or a mix.
