Overview, the visual round

The Overview on stage (demo D) went all the way to tables to stop looking home-made, and now it reads like a spreadsheet. This round goes the other way: each option leads with one picture of the fleet, and the picture is made of real data (a square is a device, a bar is a day, a dot is a restaurant). Nothing is decoration or an invented score. Every mock uses the same fleet: Orange County restaurants, T7s, kiosks, menu boards, KDS and handhelds. Hover the pictures, they have tooltips.

Current: counts, queue, restaurants table On stage

Shorthand of what stage serves at / today. Honest, fast to scan, but there is no picture of the fleet anywhere on it. Every option below keeps its good part (every number opens a filtered list) and adds the picture.

A · Device wall: every device is one square Recommended

Each restaurant is a block and each device in it is a square coloured by its state right now. Close to 500 squares fit on a laptop screen, and a bad restaurant shows up as a red clump before you've read anything. The queue next to it says what the red is.

Good at
  • The whole fleet in one look, at the level of single devices. Nothing is averaged away.
  • Scales: 1,500 devices is still one screen at 8px squares.
  • Colour means one thing everywhere: the device's state.
Bad at
  • Says nothing about trends. Yesterday's outage is gone once it's fixed.
  • Restaurant names are small, so it depends on hover and the queue.
Data
  • All of it exists today: device state, restaurant, class, active alerts. No new queries, just one more SELECT of (id, restaurant, state).

B · Treemap: the fleet as a stock-market map

Like a stock-market map: each restaurant is a rectangle sized by how many devices it has, and the colour gets stronger the worse it's doing. Inside, the dashed boxes split it by device type. Big red rectangles are the problems that affect the most devices.

Good at
  • Weighs problems by size: a 20-device restaurant in trouble takes up more of the screen than a 5-device one.
  • Very dense. Every restaurant is on screen with its name.
Bad at
  • Small restaurants turn into slivers with no room for a label.
  • Colour intensity needs a formula, which is close to the "invented score" we removed.
Data
  • Exists: device counts per restaurant and class, offline and alert counts.

C · Status page: 30 days of uptime per restaurant

Borrowed from status pages like GitHub's: a banner that says in one sentence whether things are fine, then one row of daily uptime bars per restaurant, then the incident log. Anyone who has looked at a status page already knows how to read it.

Good at
  • Shows history. A restaurant that drops every Friday shows up as a pattern.
  • You could show the same page to a restaurant owner or support as-is.
Bad at
  • The unit is a day, so an outage that started 20 minutes ago is one small bar.
  • 31 rows is long. It needs a "show all" once the fleet grows.
Data
  • device_daily_stats.online_minutes already has uptime per device per day. The incident log comes from resolved alerts.

D · Pulse: a week of the fleet, then today hour by hour

A large chart of devices online over 7 days, with rollouts, outages and crash spikes marked on it where they happened. Below it, every restaurant is a row with one cell per hour for the last 24 hours, so an outage looks like a dark run across a row.

Good at
  • Links cause and effect: "we started the v2.1.021 rollout, then the online count dipped".
  • The heatmap catches restaurants that drop off every night or at peak hours.
Bad at
  • Two charts before any worklist. On a bad day you still have to scroll to see what to do.
Data
  • Online per hour: device_state_events (the stage fleet chart already draws it). The event markers come from updates and alerts.

E · Map: where the trouble is

A hand-drawn map of Orange County (coastline, the 5, 405 and 91 freeways) with no map tiles, no API key and no Google branding. Each dot is a restaurant, sized by device count and coloured by state. Restaurants at risk pulse. We already store latitude and longitude on restaurants.

Good at
  • The most "wow" option, and it answers "is this one area?" (for example an ISP outage in Anaheim).
  • Useful for field techs: which restaurants can I visit on one trip.
Bad at
  • Clustered cities hide each other. Irvine and Santa Ana overlap at this zoom.
  • A new region (another state) needs its own outline, or real map tiles.
Data
  • Exists: restaurants.latitude/longitude. The outline is a static SVG, a few KB per region.

F · Orbit: the fleet as a clock face

Every device is a tick around the ring, grouped by restaurant, worst restaurants starting at 12 o'clock. The inner ring shows device types. Red ticks stick out further, so problems pop off the edge. Only the restaurants with problems get a label.

Good at
  • Memorable. It looks like nothing else in the product, and a demo audience will remember it.
  • Same data as the wall, so it's honest.
Bad at
  • Angles are harder to compare than rows. Takes more reading than A.
  • Past ~800 devices the ticks merge, so it needs a zoom.
Data
  • Same as A.

G · Restaurant twins: each site drawn as its hardware Surprise

Each restaurant card draws the actual devices it has: tablets, kiosk panels, the menu-board TV, the KDS screen, handhelds. A dark screen with a red dot is offline. You read it the way a support call goes: "the menu board at Taco Loco is dark".

Good at
  • Matches how support and restaurant owners talk: by device type, not serial.
  • A great sales demo. Our own hardware, on screen.
Bad at
  • Big restaurants (40+ devices) need a "+30" overflow.
  • Needs one small icon per device class, and a new class needs a drawing.
Data
  • Exists: device_class + state per device.

H · Firmware river + fleet funnel

For release days. The left chart shows 30 days of which build the T7s ran, so each rollout is a new colour spreading across it. On the right, a funnel from enrolled down to fully healthy and up to date, labelled with how many devices drop out at each step and why.

Good at
  • Rollout progress over time. "Is v2.1.021 actually landing?" is answered without opening Updates.
  • The funnel makes "healthy" countable, with every step labelled.
Bad at
  • A release-manager view more than an on-call one. Better as the Releases landing page, maybe.
Data
  • device_daily_stats.build_id (the last build seen per device per day) gives the river directly.

I · NOC wall: for the TV in the office Surprise

Not a replacement for the Overview but a second mode (/?tv=1). Always dark, huge numbers you can read across the room, one tile per restaurant that blinks when it's at risk, and a ticker of live events. It refreshes itself and has no controls.

Good at
  • Makes an outage visible without anyone opening the dashboard.
  • Cheap: the same numbers, a different stylesheet, auto-refresh.
Bad at
  • It's a mode, not an overview. It doesn't solve the main page by itself.
  • Needs a logged-in session on the TV (a read-only token link would help).
Data
  • Exists. The ticker could use the SSE stream the dashboard already has.

J · Today: what happened since midnight

A timeline for today with one lane per kind of event: outages, alerts, crashes, commands, rollouts and enrolments, with a "now" line. Restaurant opening hours are shaded. Under it, the four things worth knowing, in plain words.

Good at
  • Answers what a shift handover actually asks: "what happened today, and is it still going?"
  • Shows overlaps: the crash spike started right after the command batch.
Bad at
  • The command lane gets dense on a busy day and needs grouping into bars.
  • Shows no fleet state, only events.
Data
  • Exists: alerts, crashes (device_events), commands, updates, enrolments, all timestamped.

K · Bento: a bit of everything

A grid of tiles in different sizes, each holding a small version of one of the pictures above: the wall, the pulse, the map, the rollout rings, crashes by hour and the queue. Apple / Vercel style.

Good at
  • The most visual per square inch. It looks finished and modern.
  • You can pick the tiles from any of A to J.
Bad at
  • Close to the old 11-widget page: every tile has the same weight and there's no obvious place to start.
  • The tiles are too small to act on, so everything becomes a link somewhere else.
Data
  • Whatever the tiles need, all of it covered above.

Recommendation: A, the device wall

A is the one I'd build. It's the only option where the picture is the fleet itself rather than a summary of it. An operator sees the red clump, reads the queue next to it and clicks through, with no chart to decode first. It needs no new data, and it keeps what was good about the current stage page: the counts at the top and the queue.

What I'd borrow from the others:

  1. From D: the 7-day online chart as a thin strip under the counts, for trend.
  2. From E: a "Wall · Map" toggle on the same card. The map is too good to leave out and costs one SVG.
  3. From I: the TV mode as a separate URL. It's cheap and the office will use it.

G (twins) is the runner-up if the page is also meant for sales demos and restaurant owners. C and H fit better as the Restaurants and Releases landing pages than as the Overview. Pick a letter, or a mix like "A + map toggle", and I'll build it on stage.
