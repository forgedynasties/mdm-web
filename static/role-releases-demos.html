Role releases: where builds become what venues run

Today the Apps page is a library of every APK, with an Install button on each row. In the role model nobody installs by hand on a restaurant device. Each role has one app, a prod channel that every deployed device follows, and lab slots the testing team puts builds on. Builds arrive as a link from the software team, so the page starts with "Add build from link": paste it, and MDM downloads the APK, reads its package and version, and works out the role. Names here (prod, slot, deployed) are placeholders. Firmware releases are a per-role setting (the switch on each card). When it is on, firmware applies only to the role's devices on the Firmware client, which is our in-house hardware. Devices on the Standard client, which is stock hardware, follow the app channel only, even inside a role that has firmware switched on. Deployed devices follow prod firmware, and lab devices follow a slot. Version numbers and counts are made up. See the plan.

1 · Role board

One card per role. Each card answers the two questions the testing team has: what is prod, and how much of the fleet is on it, and what is on each lab slot. New builds wait in an inbox at the top until someone puts them on a slot or promotes them.

Where it sits in the dock

Today the dock has Apps for APKs and a separate Firmware entry whose tabs are Updates, Releases and Packages. The MDM client has its own Clients page. Option 1 puts app and firmware on one card per role, so two dock entries become one. Lock no longer needs a dock entry either, because a deployed device is locked by its role. It stays reachable from More for debug devices.

A · Apps + Firmware become Releases my pick
OverviewFleetReleasesAppsFirmwareJobsAlertsLockMore

Releases gets tabs: Roles (the board), Library (every APK, including lab tools with no role such as Thermobench), and Packages (today's OTA packages: full and incremental per source build). Today's firmware Releases tab becomes the firmware half of each role card, so the name moves up a level instead of colliding. The Clients page for the MDM client stays separate: it is our agent, not a restaurant app, and its auto-update policy already works.

B · Keep Apps and Firmware, add channels to each
OverviewFleetAppsFirmwareJobsAlertsLockMore

No relearning, but a role's prod app and prod firmware are set on two pages. "Apps" also suggests a store you install from, which is the habit this refactor removes.

Open question this raises

Deployed T7s on the old v2.0 line. 101 of the 188 live devices run a v2.0.x image, signed with the GMS key. "Deployed follows prod firmware" cannot apply to them: a v2.1 build is signed with a key they do not trust, and the migration OTA is what left the bad abl in a slot before. Should they (a) be shown as "old line, no firmware updates" and follow the app channel only, (b) get a separate frozen prod pointer on the v2.0 line, or (c) be migrated to v2.1 as a planned project of its own? My lean: (a) now, (c) as its own decision later.

My recommendation

Option 1 with dock A. The model's unit is the role, and option 1 is the only one where a role, its prod app, its prod firmware and fleet progress for both are one card. The pipeline (2) draws an order that slots do not have. The library (3) keeps the APK-store framing that this refactor is meant to retire. Lab-only tools keep a home as a Library tab, so nothing is lost.
