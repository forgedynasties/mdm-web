Search-bar dock: better icons

This is option D from the last round: a few destinations as icons and a wide search field that takes a serial, a restaurant, a page or an action. Every option below uses the same layout and differs only in the icons. The icons themselves are new: each one draws the page's job rather than a generic shape. The live figures come along as badges: the online ring wraps Fleet, the alert count sits on Alerts, and a spinner circles Actions while commands run. Hover the icons for names, and click a search field to see suggestions.

A · Refined outline

One even stroke weight (1.75) and a 24px grid for every icon, the Lucide / Linear style. The current page gets a tint and a short underline.

Good at
  • Quiet and consistent. It matches the rest of the dashboard's icons.
Bad at
  • Thin lines at 21px are the hardest of the five to tell apart at a glance.

B · Duotone

The same outlines with a soft fill in each shape's body. The fill deepens on the current page. It gives the icons weight without making them loud.

Good at
  • More presence than A while keeping its calm. It reads well in both themes.
Bad at
  • The fill is subtle, so the current page still depends on the tint behind it.

C · Outline, solid when active Recommended

The iOS tab-bar convention: every icon is an outline until it's the page you're on, then it turns solid in the brand colour with a dot underneath. "Where am I" is answered by the icon itself, not a box around it.

Good at
  • The clearest current-page signal of the five, without adding anything around the icon.
  • A pattern everyone already knows from their phone.
Bad at
  • Every icon needs a second, solid drawing, so there are two sets to maintain.

D · App tiles

Each destination is a coloured rounded tile with a white glyph, like a phone home screen or the macOS dock: coral Overview, periwinkle Fleet, amber Actions, red Alerts. Tiles lift on hover.

Good at
  • The most visual and the most "ours", using the brand coral and periwinkle.
  • Easy to find by colour, even out of the corner of your eye.
Bad at
  • Loud for a tool open all day, and a red tile for Alerts looks like an alert even when there isn't one.

E · Icon over a tiny label

Refined icons with their names underneath in small text, so nothing depends on hovering. A little wider than the others.

Good at
  • No guessing: new staff read the names, and everyone else reads the shapes.
Bad at
  • It takes about 80px more width, and with the frequent pages added it starts to crowd the search field.

Recommendation: C

C: outline icons that turn solid on the current page. It keeps the search-bar dock calm like A, but the current page is obvious without a box around it, and it's a pattern everyone knows from their phone. It also leaves the badges (online ring, alert count, spinner) as the only colour on the dock, so they stand out. D is the one to pick if you want the dock to feel like a product rather than a tool. E is the safe choice if new staff get lost. Pick a letter and I'll build it.
