Open in lab: what it looks like, end to end

Chosen 14 Oct: the separate lab view, entered with A (timed, with a reason).

This expands option 2 from the device page demos. The device page stays the short "is it doing its job" view. Lab work happens on a separate lab view at its own URL, which looks different on purpose so nobody mistakes it for the normal page. Below are the four moments: the button, getting in (three ways to choose from), the lab view itself, and what happens when lab ends. Device, venue, versions and output are made up.

2 · Getting in: three ways

The real decision is when the device stops behaving like a restaurant device: when you open the lab, for as long as a tab is open, or only when you do something that changes it. Switch between them.

Why (shown on the device and venue pages, and in the audit log)
No dialog
The lab view opens straight away. The device goes into lab when the page loads, and stays there while any lab tab for it is open; the page pings the server every 30 s. Two minutes after the last tab closes, lab ends.

What breaks: a laptop lid closes during a 6-hour soak test and the device snaps back to prod and lock mid-test. A test that needs the device left alone overnight cannot be done at all.
Lab view opens read-only
Opening the lab view changes nothing. You can see logcat, the screen and what is installed. The first action that changes the device (a shell, a slot, an install) asks the question from A, "for how long and why", and only then takes it out of prod.

Good for support people who mostly look. One more step for the testing team, who always act.
A is my pick. It is the 24-hour expiry you already chose, asked once at the door. The reason is what makes "why is Table 14 unlocked?" answerable by anyone.
C is worth having later if support staff start using lab only to look. It is A with the question moved to the first change, so it can be added without redoing anything.

4 · When lab ends

By the timer, by End lab now, or by removing the device from its venue. The reconciler then puts it back without anyone remembering to.

Back to prodThe prod package (aio.app.nugget 4.2.1) is installed and the internal package removed, inside the venue's quiet window. Variants are separate packages, so nothing is ever downgraded in place.
Cleaned and lockedLab apps installed in this session are removed. The device locks to Nugget again. The shell transcript stays on the device's timeline with who, when and why.
Visible meanwhileThe device page shows a "In lab until 10:40" banner. The venue page shows "1 device in lab", so nobody at AIO is surprised by an unlocked table.

Settled by your answers (14 Oct)

No downgrade problem. Lab columns are package variants, so leaving lab swaps packages instead of downgrading one. Firmware in lab is off-venue only. Who sees the button: testing and firmware teams and super admins, my lean, not yet confirmed.

My recommendation

Use entry A with this lab view, and compare it with option 1 on the device page demos. The two differ only in where the lab tools live: a Debug tab on the same page, or a separate lab page. A separate page is clearer for the restaurant-facing audience you are rolling out to, and it gives lab work room for a real terminal. That room matters, because the shell becomes a live session. If you prefer this, I would switch my device page pick from option 1 to option 2 with entry A.
