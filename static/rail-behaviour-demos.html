Rail behaviour — opening, closing, and what happens when you navigate

Each mock is live: hover the left edge, and click a nav row to "navigate". The content and the child rail change as they would on the real thing, so the question each option answers is what should the app rail do at the moment you arrive somewhere new. A is what is on stage.

A · Snap shut on arrival on stage now

Hover opens it, moving away closes it, and clicking a destination closes it at once — the new page arrives with the rail already folded. Good: the page you asked for gets the whole screen immediately, and the rule is one sentence. Bad: if you meant to click the row below, you have to re-open; and the close fires while the page is still loading, so the rail appears to flinch away before the new content lands.

↓ hover the rail, then click a row

B · Stay open until you leave it my pick

Clicking a destination navigates but leaves the rail open; it folds when the pointer leaves the rail, or after a short grace period if the pointer never moves. Good: two or three hops in a row cost one gesture, which is how nav actually gets used, and nothing moves while the page is still swapping. Bad: the new page is briefly under the drawer, so the first thing you see is covered by ~200px until you move the mouse.

↓ click a row — the rail stays until you move off it

C · Remembered, not hovered

Hover does nothing. The brand mark toggles the rail open or shut, and that choice sticks across pages and sessions. Good: nothing ever moves unless you ask; the best option for a screen someone watches all day, and for anyone who finds hover menus twitchy. Bad: navigation costs an extra click when the rail is shut, and a person who never finds the toggle lives with icons forever.

↓ click the logo to toggle; hover does nothing

D · Open on arrival, fold after a beat

Every navigation lands with the rail open for about a second, then it folds by itself. Good: you always see where you are in the nav right after you move, which is the moment the highlight matters. Bad: motion the person did not ask for, on every single page load — the kind of thing that is charming twice and irritating after that.

↓ click a row — the rail opens itself, then folds

Pick: B. A is correct about giving the page the screen, but it optimises the rarest case — one hop, then reading — and punishes the common one. B keeps the drawer under the pointer that is already there, and the cover is temporary by definition because the drawer closes the moment you move toward the content you asked for. C is worth keeping as a preference on top of B rather than instead of it.
