Where Kiosk lives, and what belongs beside it

Kiosk is now at /kiosk and settable per venue, but it is still behind More, and it is the thing operators touch most. The question is not only "promote it" — it is what is it the first of, because a sixth dock entry should name a family, not a single page.

1 · What is actually in this family

Everything in the product that decides how a device behaves when nobody is touching it — as opposed to the nouns (devices, apps, venues, builds) or the answers (jobs, alerts):

ThingWhere it is nowWhat it decides
Kiosk/kiosk + venue page + device pagewhich app the tablet is locked to, and whether the on-device exit code works
Boot logo / splashretired from the UI, 11 Oct — backend still wiredwas: what the device shows while it starts. Not part of this family any more.
Guest Wi-Fi & tablevenue pagewhat a guest sees on the tablet's home and lock screens
Schedules/scheduleswhat happens on a timer — nightly reboots, recurring checks
Check-in intervalburied in Settingshow often a device reports, which is the fleet's whole latency budget
Alert rules/alert-configwhat the fleet shouts about — arguably belongs to Alerts, not here
Wireless adb/adbnothing about behaviour — it is a diagnostic door. Does not belong
So the family is real, and it has about five members.

Kiosk, guest experience, schedules and the check-in interval are all answers to "what do these devices do on their own". They are spread across four pages and two settings screens today, which is why kiosk ended up with three homes in the first place.

Kiosk if it stays one page, Policies if it becomes the family — with the Kiosk tab first and selected by default, so the word people look for is still the first thing on screen.

3 · Three ways to do it
Option A — Kiosk as the sixth dock entry, nothing else moves

Promote the page as it is. Smallest change; the rest of the family stays scattered.

Good at An hour. Puts the most-used page one click from anywhere and names it the word operators use.
Bad at Boot logo stays under Firmware, where it has nothing to do with rollouts; schedules and the check-in interval stay lost. The family problem is untouched.
Option B — a Policies entry, Kiosk as its first tabmy pick

One dock entry for the family, tabbed like Firmware now is. Landing tab is Kiosk, so the common case is identical to option A.

Kiosk tab — the rules, which venues they cover, who was taken out on site, and the fleet count.
Good at Gives the five scattered settings one home and a shared shape, the same pattern Firmware just got — so the product gets more consistent rather than more varied. Kiosk still lands first, so nothing gets slower for the common case.
Bad at "Policies" is the weakest word on the page, and moving Boot logo out of Firmware breaks whatever muscle memory exists. Two pages (boot logo, schedules) have to be re-homed, not just linked.
Option C — fold it into the venue, and drop the dock entry

Kiosk is a venue decision, so the venue page is the only place it is set; the fleet-wide list lives under Fleet as "Kiosk status". No new dock entry at all.

Good at The purest version of the noun model — kiosk is a property, not a place, and we already put it on the venue page. Keeps the dock at five.
Bad at "Which tablets are unlocked right now" is a fleet question that then has no obvious home, and lab devices belong to no venue. It also contradicts the request: the page people use most would be the one page with no entry.
4 · What I would do
 StepSize
1Add the sixth dock entry now, labelled Kiosk, pointing at /kiosk — the page is ready and this is the request~1h
2Give /kiosk the tab strip: Kiosk · Guest · Schedules, reusing the Firmware pattern. Entry stays labelled Kiosk while it is the only full tab~half a day
3Move Schedules out of Control into a tab; drop it from the directory~2h
4Surface the check-in interval there instead of in Settings, where nobody finds it~2h
5Rename the entry to Policies only if the tabs make "Kiosk" feel wrong — decide when you can see it, not nowminutes

That order gets the request done first and lets the family assemble behind it, and it defers the naming decision until there is something to look at. Wireless adb stays out — it is a diagnostic door, not device behaviour, and it belongs with the fleet. Alert rules stay out — they describe what the fleet tells you, which is Alerts' job.
