Collapsed child rail — what tells you it is still there

Reaching for the app rail expands it to 196px and squeezes the page's own rail down to a 56px column of initials. The swap is width-neutral, so nothing under it reflows — but for the two seconds your pointer is on the nav, that column has to still read as navigation you can go back to, not as decoration. What is shipped now is initials plus an e-resize cursor. The title-and-chevron stub that was there before is out.

A · Initials only shipped now

Nothing but the items, centred, two letters each. The current page's item keeps its accent fill, which is the one thing that survives the squeeze and says "this column is the page's own nav". Good: silent, no chrome, nothing to misread. Bad: on a rail whose initials are weak (Al Ne In Co) it can read as a column of tags.

B · Edge tab my pick

A small handle on the rail's outer edge, vertically centred — the same grammar as a drawer pull. It says "this panel is folded, push here" without spending a word or a row. Good: one affordance for the whole rail, reads instantly, survives any set of initials. Bad: one more element on screen; it must not look clickable-but-dead, so it also opens the rail on click, not only on hover.

C · Vertical name

The rail's own name set sideways down the top of the strip — COLLECTIONS, SETTINGS, ALERTS. Good: names the thing, which initials never do, and costs no horizontal room. Bad: rotated type is slow to read and is the one pattern on this dashboard that appears nowhere else; long names clip.

D · Three-letter cap

A single hairline-separated cap at the top: COL, SET, ALR. Horizontal, so it is read at a glance, and the hairline makes the strip a panel rather than a list of chips. Good: cheapest thing that both names and frames the rail. Bad: abbreviations are a small vocabulary to learn, and a wrong one is worse than none.

Bad: only pays off if you hover, and the pointer is on the app rail at that moment, not here.

F · Accent seam

No text and no handle: the collapsed strip grows a 2px accent seam on its outer edge, the same colour as the active item. Good: quietest of the lot, and it ties the strip to the page it belongs to. Bad: a coloured edge is decoration until you have learned it; it says "something is here", not "this folds open".

Pick: B, with E's tooltips. The tab is the only option that says folded, and it comes back rather than just naming the strip — and because it is on the edge, it is the nearest thing to the pointer on its way back from the app rail.
