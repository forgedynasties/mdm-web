Actions — a fresh take on the UX

The page works, but it’s configure-in-the-dark, then go hunting: you build an action without seeing who it’ll hit, press Send, then scroll to a flat list and click into a plain table to watch it land. Four concepts below re-shape that flow. They’re live — click around.

Concept 1 · Launchpadinteractive
A live “blast radius” panel — see who you’ll hit before you fire recommended
Add a third pane to the builder. As you pick an action and target, it computes the real impact now: how many devices, how many are online vs. will queue, and action-specific risks (rebooting low battery, uninstalling from devices that don’t have the app). The Send button stops saying “Send action” and starts saying exactly what’s about to happen.

Today’s gap: All Devices is an abstraction. You can reboot 40 tablets — 6 of them at 4% battery — and the only feedback before Send is a generic warning line.

The whole panel is derived from data the page already has (online state, battery, per-device packages for uninstall). No new backend — it’s the same browse-devices query, summarised. The Send label + hint remove every “wait, what did I just target?” moment.
Concept 2 · Mission Controlanimated
Watch it land — a live rollup instead of a flat delivery table
Right after Send, the builder collapses into this. A progress ring fills as acknowledgements stream in over the WebSocket you already have; the roll-call flips each device pending → running → done in place. Filter to just failures and retry them in one click — no per-row hunting.
Today’s gap: the detail page is a static table that polls every 30s. For a 40-device reboot you refresh and squint at status badges.

Concept 3 · Recipes
One-tap for the actions you run constantly
Most fleet actions repeat: nightly kiosk screenshots, rebooting a specific restaurant, re-installing the current POS build. A strip of recipes above the builder turns a 6-click sequence into one. “Re-run last” is automatic; the rest are saved presets (action + target + payload baked in).

Concept 4 · Activity timelinelive progress
History that shows motion, grouped by time
The flat history list becomes a timeline. In-flight actions show a live progress bar and a pulsing “live” marker; finished ones collapse to a calm summary. Grouped Today / Yesterday so recent work is scannable.

Recommendation: ship Concept 1 (Launchpad) first — it’s the biggest confidence win and reuses existing data. Concept 2 (Mission Control) pairs with it as the post-send view. Recipes & the timeline are lower-effort polish on top.
