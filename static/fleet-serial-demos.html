The serial number on the fleet card

Two card layouts ship today — Classic, where the serial is the card's link, and the newer New layout, where the model leads and the serial drops to a quiet mono line. Two more take it further. All four on the same fleet, so the only thing that changes is how much the serial is allowed to do.

What the serial has to do
Match the stickerA technician holding the device must find its row without reading anything else.
Be quotableSupport reads it aloud, or a customer pastes it into chat. Copy has to be one click.
Be searchablePaste a serial, get the device — no name, no model, no guessing the page.
ReconcileA column of serials compared against an inventory sheet, by eye.

Options

Classic Deployed

Good at: the serial is the card's own link — it is the only identity on the row, so the sticker, the URL and the row are the same string. Nothing to learn. Bad at: a long serial is the whole headline, so the fleet reads as a wall of near-identical mono; scanning for Front counter means reading past the serial every time, and the model only appears as a secondary line. No copy — you select the text by hand.

A · New Deployed

Good at: the human handle leads — nickname or model — so a fleet that is named well reads straight down. Status, last-seen and the badges all sit on that line, and RAM/Temp/Cycles get a tidy column. Bad at: the serial is demoted to an 11 px muted mono in the second line — the quietest thing on a card where it is the most stable identifier. It is also the least copyable: nothing on the card takes it for you, and it is the field support asks for.

B · Serial leads My pick

Good at: quoting and matching. The serial is the headline, in mono, split into the quiet batch prefix and a bold distinctive tail — AT070AABU00281 — so two similar units are told apart by the last five characters, which is what people actually read out. Copy is one click, and the name and model drop to a quiet second line where they still identify the device. Bad at: it demotes the name, so a well-named fleet loses its most human handle; and every row now starts with same-length mono, so the list looks more uniform at a glance.

C · Serial column

Good at: reconciling. The serial gets its own aligned, tabular-figures column, so a page of serials lines up digit-for-digit against a spreadsheet, and the header sorts by it. Copy on hover, plus copy-the-whole-selection in the toolbar. Bad at: it is a list, not a card — the type icon and vitals shrink, so it reads less like a fleet and more like an inventory report. Best as a second view on the fleet page (a toggle beside the existing New/Classic switch), not as the only card.
