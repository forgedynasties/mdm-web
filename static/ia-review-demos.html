2 · What is wrong with it
Two entries for the same page, twice.

The dock's Fleet and the directory's Devices are both /devices. The dock's Apps and the directory's App library are both /apps. Two names for one page teaches people the nav is unreliable.

"Deploy" is five pages for one idea.

/updates renders a "Releases" section and a "Rollouts" section; /releases renders an "Updates in progress" section. Each page shows a slice of the other, so neither is the answer to "what firmware is going out and where has it got to". Packages, Clients and Boot logo are also "something we ship to devices".

Kiosk is a page called Manage.

The route is /manage, the label is Kiosk, and the actual kiosk state lives in three places: that page's rules, the device page's toggle, and the Devices bulk bar. The most safety-critical thing the product does — locking a tablet to one app in front of a customer — has no single home.

The directory is a filing cabinet, not a map.

Ten Admin entries ranked equally, so Settings and Alert report look like peers. Nothing says which pages you use daily and which you touched once during setup.

What is already right, and should be protected

The noun-first direction of the last few days works: installs start from the app, diagnostics start from a device selection, and Jobs is where work is watched. The dock's five entries are the right five. The fix below is mostly deleting and renaming, not adding.

3 · The model

Four nouns, and every verb hangs off the noun it affects. Two more pages exist only to answer "what is happening" and "what needs me".

NounThe pageWhat you do there
A device, or a set of themFleetfind it, see it, act on it — shell, screenshot, logs, reboot, kiosk, wipe. Sets are made by filtering.
An appAppswho has it, which version, install where it is missing
A placeVenuesthe devices at a restaurant, its guest Wi-Fi, its kiosk policy
A buildFirmwarewhat exists, what is rolling out, where it has got to
—Jobseverything sent: in flight, stuck, finished
—Alertswhat the fleet is telling you without being asked

Kiosk is deliberately not a noun: it is a property of a device and a policy of a venue, which is why it currently has three homes and no owner.

5 · Kiosk — the one that needs a decision

Locking a tablet to one app is the most visible thing this product does and the most dangerous to get wrong. Today it is split across three places with no owner.

 1 · Kiosk is a page2 · Kiosk is a venue policy3 · Kiosk is a device property
Where you set it/kiosk — rules, exits, allowlistsVenue page — "this restaurant's tablets run X"Device page + bulk bar only
Fits becauseRules are fleet-wide and need one homeIt is how the business thinks: a venue has an ordering appIt is literally a per-device setting
Breaks becauseA page for one setting; you still set it per device elsewhereLab and spare devices belong to no venueNo overview — "which tablets are unlocked right now" has no page

I would do 2 with 1 as the overview: the venue page is where you say what its tablets run, because that is the sentence the business actually says; /kiosk stays as the fleet-wide view of who is locked, who exited on site, and which rule put them there. The device page keeps the override. What has to stop is three places that can each silently disagree.

before→Manage→write a rule→hope it matches→check devices individually
after→Venues→the venue→Kiosk: AIO Order — 14 tablets
Find out whether what you sent worked
before→Actions→scroll history→read "delivered"→believe a lie

The through-line: every verb belongs to a noun, and every page is either a noun or an answer ("what is happening", "what needs me"). Judged that way the dock is already right, Apps and Jobs are now right, Fleet is nearly right, and the remaining work is Kiosk, Firmware, and deleting the console.
