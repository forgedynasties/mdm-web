Readout panel — how it should look

Same panel, same numbers, four typographic treatments with icons. The complaint about what is deployed is fair: every label is uppercase 800-weight, the values are 760, so the column shouts at you and nothing sits above anything else. Each option below drops the weight, adds a glyph per row, and picks a different way to line the numbers up. Panel width, content and behaviour are unchanged — this is styling only.

Option A
Quiet labels, icon per row, medium-weight numbers my pick

Labels drop to sentence case at 10.5px/600 and stop shouting; values go from 760 to 500 at 16px with the unit in small grey — the number reads as data, not as a headline. A line icon sits left of each pair, so the column scans by shape.

Good at Calmest of the four while keeping the numbers big enough to read across the desk. Icons carry the meaning so the labels can afford to be quiet.
Bad at Values are left-aligned under their labels, so digits shift a pixel or two as they change width.
Option B
One line per metric, numbers right-aligned on hairlines

Icon, label, number — all on one row, separated by hairline rules, numbers flush right so they never move as they change. Half the height of A.

Good at Nothing jumps: fixed rows, flush-right digits. Compact enough that the panel could narrow to ~165px and give the plot back some width.
Bad at Numbers are small (13.5px) — this is a readout you lean in for. The charge note has to hang under its row.
Option C
Series-coloured icons, light numerals

Each icon takes its series colour from the chart — battery indigo, temp red, RAM green — so the panel and the lines are obviously the same thing. Numbers at 18px but weight 400.

Each metric in its own soft chip: icon, label, number. Deliberately quiet, no big numerals at all.

My pick: A, and if the digits shifting bothers you, A with B's flush-right numbers is a two-line change. C is worth it only if the All tab is where you spend time. Whichever you pick, the timestamp line loses its bold and the uppercase labels go — that part is the actual fix.
